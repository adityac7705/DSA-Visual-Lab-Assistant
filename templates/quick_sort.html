QUICK SORT

What is Quick Sort?

Quick Sort Quick Sort is a highly efficient, comparison-based, divide-and-conquer sorting algorithm. It works by selecting a pivot element from the array and dividing the remaining elements into two sub-arrays: elements less than the pivot go to the left, and elements greater than the pivot go to the right. The choice of pivot and the partitioning process are key to its efficiency. Quick Sort is an in-place algorithm, meaning it doesn’t require extra space for another array, which makes it memory-efficient.

The algorithm then recursively applies the same process to the left and right sub-arrays until the entire array is sorted. During partitioning, two pointers—Left and Right—move toward each other, swapping elements that are on the wrong side of the pivot. Finally, the pivot is placed in its correct sorted position. Quick Sort has an average time complexity of O(n log n), though in the worst case it can be O(n²) if the pivot choices are poor. Its efficiency and in-place nature make it one of the most widely used sorting algorithms.

Steps

  • Step1: Choose Pivot – usually the first element.
  • Step2: Initialize Pointers – Left after pivot, Right at end.
  • Step3: Move Pointers
    1. Left → stops at ≥ pivot
    2. Right → stops at ≤ pivot
  • Step4: Swap Elements if Left ≤ Right.
  • Step5: Repeat until pointers cross.
  • Step6: Place Pivot at Right pointer’s position.
  • Step7: Recursively Sort Left and Right subarrays.
